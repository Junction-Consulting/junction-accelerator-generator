# Yukon Alpine Adventures: Accelerator Implementation Plan

## Meta

## Goal
Your 2027 season is set: five dated expeditions from June 24 to September 11, six guests each, which is 30 seats at $3,900 to $5,300 per person. Since The New York Times feature came out this November, you have gone from about one inquiry a month to four or five a week. The 90 days of this plan run through December, January and February, which is exactly when your guests start talking to you and then commit. The goal is to turn that attention into confirmed seats without adding hours you don't have.

By the end of February, anyone who finds you should be able to see every 2027 departure in one place with its dates, price, difficulty and spots left, and send you a booking request in one step that tells you which trip they want and how experienced they are. Your screening conversation stays exactly as it is. You will also know which requests came from Instagram, Google, the article, ChatGPT or a friend, because right now every booking arrives as an email and none of it is measured.

Alongside that, you'll move from posting about once every two weeks to two or three times a week, using a content system built around the one full day a week you have in the off-season. Your four itinerary PDFs will become web pages that Google and AI tools can read and cite, so the trip-planning searches that already sent you your first guest in 2025 keep working for you.

The order matters. The booking path comes first because the inquiries are arriving now. Content runs the whole 90 days because the booking window is open the whole 90 days. Itinerary pages and search work come in January, once the booking path is in place. This plan has no paid advertising in it. Your past Meta and Google campaigns ran without any way to connect spend to bookings, and that gap gets fixed here first. The one real cost is the creator trip in Tactic 2: giving a seat on a late-season departure to a content creator is a marketing expense of up to one full trip price plus your share of the flight, and you should plan for it that way.

## Strategic Positioning
Almost every guided hiking company in the Yukon sells the same handful of places, and the busiest ones sell out the Tombstones first. You built Yukon Alpine Adventures to go where those groups don't: fly-in, off-trail routes through Kusawa, Asi Keyi, the Boundary Ranges and the headwaters of the Takhini, where you are far more likely to meet caribou, Dall sheep or a grizzly than another hiking party. You cap groups at six, cook real food in the backcountry, photograph the whole trip, and you have built every route with the consent and involvement of the First Nations whose land you travel on. That is a rare combination, and it is why a New York Times writer who couldn't get into the Tombstones ended up with a better story on your trip.

Your guests are mostly women in their mid-30s to early 50s, mostly Canadian, who already hike and want something harder and more remote than a marked trail. They plan their whole Yukon trip around your expedition, not the other way around, and they ask a lot of questions before they commit: about fitness, bears, weather and what the days actually look like. Your marketing should answer those questions in public, through itinerary pages, carousels and FAQs, so that the people who reach your booking form already know they are a fit.

This is not a trip for first-time hikers looking for a scenic day out, or for anyone who wants lodges and marked trails. Saying that clearly protects your screening process and makes the right guests more confident, not less.

> **Your Strategic Positioning**
> Yukon Alpine Adventures takes small groups of experienced hikers by floatplane into remote, trail-free Yukon mountains that few people ever see, on routes built in partnership with the First Nations whose land they cross. You come home having walked with caribou, eaten well and been looked after by a guide who knows this country intimately.

## Tactic 1: A Clear Path From Inquiry to Booking
Phase: Foundation
Short Label: Booking Path
Subtitle: A departures page, a booking request form and simple tracking so interested hikers can take the first step and you can see where they came from.

### What We Heard From You
Right now every booking starts as an email, followed by "multiple weeks of back and forth" while you answer questions about fitness, bears and logistics. You want to keep screening guests, because some of your trips mean carrying a heavy pack far from help, and on the coaching call you were open to anything that helps: "I'll take every card I can get." Every trip page currently sends people to a general contact page with your email and phone number, and you told us you have no reliable way to tell which of your marketing efforts are producing bookings.

### What This Is
A booking path that feels like booking without giving up your screening call. You'll add a 2027 departures page that lists every trip with its dates, price, difficulty and spots left, and a booking request form that asks the questions you already ask by email. When someone submits it, they land on a thank-you page that tells them what happens next, and that page view is counted in Google Analytics and Meta as a booking request.

### Why It Matters
People who can see a date and press a button feel they have taken a step, and more of them take it than will write a cold email. Your best inquiries already come with context and questions, while blank ones rarely convert, so a form that asks for that context gives you better leads as well as more of them. With requests counted, you'll finally be able to compare channels before you spend another dollar on ads.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Show What's Available

1. **Build a 2027 departures page**
   - In Squarespace, open the Pages panel and click **+**. Under Showcase, choose **Calendar**, pick a layout and name the page "2027 Departures".
   - Click the new page, then click **+** in the side panel to add an event. Add one event per departure: Kusawa Territorial Park (June 24-30), The Boundary Ranges (July 8-15), Asi Keyi (August 3-11), Headwaters of the Takhini (August 24-31) and Where The Light Lingers (September 4-11).
   - For each event, set the start date and the end date. Set the end time to 11:59 pm, because an event that ends at midnight shows on two days.
   - Add the trip's best photo as the **Featured image** under the event's settings, in the Content tab.
   - Open the page's settings (the gear icon in the Pages panel) and set **Default event view** to **List**. A month grid opens on the current month, so a visitor in December would see a grey, empty calendar before finding June. The list shows every departure at once.
   - Edit the calendar section (pencil icon), switch **Show Time** off and **Show Thumbnails** on, and set a Date Style that shows the start and end dates.
   - Add the page to your main navigation directly under Expeditions, so it is one click from the homepage.

2. **Put the answers guests ask first on every departure**
   - In each event's settings, write an **Excerpt** of two or three lines: dates, price, challenge rating and spots left. For example: "June 24-30, 2027 · $3,900 CAD per person · Green circle · 4 of 6 spots left."
   - Add a **Source URL** pointing to the full trip page, so each event links back to the details.
   - Add a line to each trip page and excerpt that explains what "+ transaction fees" means in dollars or as a percentage, so nobody hits a surprise at payment.
   - Add North of Dakwäkäda as a short text section below the list, with a line inviting people to request custom dates.
   - Every time a guest confirms, update the spots left on that event the same day. Seeing "2 of 6 spots left" is one of the most persuasive things a guest can read in January.

3. **Point every "Start your adventure" button at the departures page**
   - On each trip page (Kusawa, The Boundary Ranges, Asi Keyi, Headwaters of the Takhini, Where The Light Lingers, North of Dakwäkäda) and on the Expeditions overview, click **Edit**, click the button, and change its link from the contact page to the 2027 Departures page.
   - Change the button text to "Request to book".
   - Leave the contact page in place for general questions, and add one line to it: "Ready to pick a trip? See 2027 departures and request your spot." with a link.

#### Part B: Turn Email Into a Booking Request

4. **Build the booking request form**
   - On the 2027 Departures page, click **Edit** and add a new section below the calendar section. You can't add blocks inside the calendar section itself, only above or below it.
   - Add a **Form** block. Click the pencil icon to edit it and build these fields:
     - **Name** and **Email**
     - **Which expedition?**: a dropdown listing each trip with its dates, plus "North of Dakwäkäda or custom dates"
     - **How many guests?**: a dropdown from 1 to 6
     - **Your backpacking experience**: a dropdown with "I hike but haven't backpacked", "A few overnight trips" and "Multi-day trips, including off-trail"
     - **Anything you're nervous about or want to ask?**: a text area
     - **How did you hear about us?**: a dropdown with The New York Times article, Instagram, Google search, ChatGPT or another AI tool, Travel Yukon, a friend, the Adventure Club newsletter, and Other
   - Make Name, Email, expedition and experience required. Leave the rest optional so the form stays quick.
   - Set the button text to "Send my booking request".
   - Under the form's **Storage** settings, keep email notifications on so each request reaches your inbox the way inquiries do now.

5. **Create a thank-you page that sets expectations**
   - Add a new page named "Booking Request Received" with the URL slug `/request-received`. Put it in the Not Linked section of the Pages panel so it doesn't appear in your navigation.
   - Write three short paragraphs: you'll reply personally within two days, you'll chat about their experience to match them with the right trip, and no payment is taken until you've both agreed it's a good fit.
   - Add a link to your Challenge Ratings page and your FAQs so they have something useful to read while they wait.
   - Back on the departures page, edit the form block, open **Content**, then **Post-Submit**, choose **Redirect** and enter `/request-received`. Save.
   - Submit a test request yourself and check that you land on the thank-you page and receive the email.

6. **Add a "Request to book" button to the site header**
   - Open the header editor (click **Edit**, then **Edit Site Header**), open **Elements** and switch on **Button**.
   - Set the button text to "Request to book" and link it to the 2027 Departures page.
   - Check on your phone that the button shows in the mobile menu. Most of your trip planners will see the site on a phone first.

#### Part C: See Where Requests Come From

7. **Count booking requests in Google Analytics**
   - Open Google Analytics, go to **Admin**, then **Data display**, then **Events**, and click **Create event**.
   - Name the new event `booking_request`. Set the conditions to: `event_name` equals `page_view`, and `page_location` contains `request-received`. Save.
   - Go to **Admin**, then **Data display**, then **Key events**, click **New key event** and enter `booking_request`.
   - Submit one more test request the next day and check that `booking_request` appears under **Reports**, then **Engagement**, then **Events**. New events can take up to a day to show.

8. **Count booking requests in Meta**
   - Your Meta Pixel is already installed. Open Meta Events Manager, choose your pixel, then go to **Custom conversions** and click **Create custom conversion**.
   - Name it "Booking request", set the rule to **URL contains** `request-received`, and choose the **Lead** category.
   - This means that if you ever run trip-specific ads again, Meta can optimise for real booking requests instead of clicks.

9. **Keep a simple request log**
   - Each time a request arrives, add one row to a spreadsheet: date, name, trip, how they heard about you, and status (replied, call booked, confirmed, not a fit).
   - The "How did you hear about us?" answer is your most reliable source data, because Google Analytics can't see what happens in your email conversations.
   - At the end of each month, count requests and confirmed bookings by source. That count is what tells you whether ads, content or partnerships deserve your money next year.

#### Part D: Decide on Deposits

10. **Decide whether to offer a deposit option before spring**
   - Option 1 is what you have now, plus the new form: guests request a spot, you screen them, and you send the payment request yourself. No new software and no new fees.
   - Option 2 is a booking system with deposits, which you raised on the call. FareHarbor has no monthly fee. It adds a booking fee of around 6% to the guest's total at checkout, with card processing on top, and some multi-day operators report that its date setup takes care to get right.
   - The deposit model you described works with Option 2: the guest puts down a deposit online, you hold your screening conversation, and you refund in full anyone who isn't a fit.
   - Look at your request log after six weeks. If people are dropping away between the request and your reply, a deposit option is worth testing for the 2028 season. If they aren't, keep things simple for 2027.

### Tips
- Keep the Excerpt line for each departure in the same order every time (dates, price, rating, spots), so people can compare trips at a glance.
- When a trip is down to its last two spots, say so in an Instagram Story and link the departures page. Real scarcity is honest urgency.
- Reply to new requests from a saved email that you personalise in the first two lines. You already do this for your post-trip emails, and it keeps replies fast on guiding days.
- Don't run ads again until you have at least a month of requests counted by source. Then you'll know what a request is worth before you pay for one.

## Tactic 2: A Repeatable Content System for Reels, Carousels and an Annual Creator Trip
Phase: Momentum
Short Label: Content System
Subtitle: A weekly posting rhythm built from a clip library, plus one planned creator partnership a year that feeds your spring booking push.

### What We Heard From You
Your content works: Reels average about 4,600 views on an account of around 1,350 followers, and your collaboration with Down Dirt Roads and Air North was your most successful campaign. What gets in the way is time and second-guessing. You've spent hours in Final Cut Pro on a single Reel, you don't want to put "slop" out there or turn Instagram into a sales channel, and when ideas run dry you run out of time. Trending audio is "a line I won't cross," and that's fine. Facebook just mirrors Instagram for you, and you haven't tried YouTube or TikTok.

### What This Is
A content system that removes the decisions. You'll choose five content categories once, keep a library of short clips filed by category, and turn them into Reels in about 15 minutes each using Instagram's Edits app. Carousels carry your trip details to the people who already follow you. The same Reels go to YouTube Shorts and TikTok, where people search for trips. Once a year, one late-season departure becomes a creator trip whose big posts and giveaway run in the spring booking window.

### Why It Matters
Instagram now shows Reels to people who are likely to be interested, whether or not they follow you, so posting more often means more chances to reach new hikers. Shares count far more than likes. You are posting about once every two weeks during the months when your guests are deciding, and every week without a post is a week someone researching the Yukon doesn't find you. A system you can run on your admin day is what makes two or three posts a week possible for a business of one.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Set Up the System

1. **Choose your five content categories**
   - Write these five categories at the top of a note on your phone, so you never start from a blank page:
     - **Come with me**: a floatplane drop-off, a morning in camp, a ridge walk
     - **The land**: one place at a time (Kusawa, Asi Keyi, the Boundary Ranges, the Takhini headwaters)
     - **How we do it**: packing, bear safety, off-trail travel, backcountry cooking from your newsletter recipes
     - **Guest moments**: first caribou, a summit, camp dinner, shared with permission
     - **Plan your trip**: itineraries, dates, what's included, who each trip suits
   - Aim for four in five posts from the first four categories. Plan your trip is the only category that sells directly, and carousels carry most of it.
   - For every post, ask one question before you publish: would someone send this to their hiking group chat? That is the share you're after.

2. **Build your clip library**
   - Make one folder for each category on your computer or cloud drive, and a sixth called "Unsorted".
   - Spend one admin day going through last summer's footage, including the Down Dirt Roads content and the trip with The New York Times writer and photographer. Drop 3 to 10 second clips into the category folders.
   - From now on, on every trip, film short clips as you go: boots on scree, the plane leaving, a pot boiling, a caribou on the skyline. You won't know what they're for when you film them, and that's fine.
   - Only use clips of guests' faces with their permission. Add a line to your pre-trip package asking for it.

3. **Learn a 15-minute Reel workflow in Edits**
   - Install Instagram's free Edits app on your phone and sign in with your Yukon Alpine Adventures Instagram account.
   - Make your first Reel from five to eight clips from one category folder. Use the app's timeline to trim them, add a short line of on-screen text at the start ("A part of the Yukon almost nobody sees"), and a voiceover or a piece of music you like.
   - Set a timer for 15 minutes. Most Reels should be finished when it goes off. Save the three-hour edits for the season launch video and the season wrap.
   - Keep using Final Cut Pro for the one or two bigger videos each year, and Edits for everything else.

#### Part B: Make the Content

4. **Make the behind-the-scenes Reel on The New York Times trip**
   - Use your footage from that trip to make a 30 to 45 second Reel along the lines of "You read the article. Here's what that trip was actually like," or "Everyone goes to the Tombstones. Here's where we went instead, and why."
   - Record a short voiceover in your own words. Mention the article in the caption and tag the writer and photographer if they're on Instagram.
   - Pin it to the top of your Instagram grid for the whole booking window.
   - Use it in your next Adventure Club newsletter and as a blog post, as you planned.

5. **Post one itinerary carousel per expedition**
   - Make one carousel for each 2027 trip, using photos you already have, in Canva or in the Instagram app.
   - Tell it as a story, one slide per stage: the floatplane in, the first camp, the hardest day, the wildlife, the last night, and a final slide with the dates, price, challenge rating and "Request to book: link in bio".
   - Look at Pacific Sands and Dani Lister's carousels before you start. Both add a short line of text to each slide so people keep swiping.
   - Post one carousel a week, Kusawa first because it suits the widest range of hikers, and finish with Asi Keyi.

6. **Keep a weekly posting rhythm**
   - On your admin day, make two Reels and one carousel, and schedule them for the week in Meta Business Suite (business.facebook.com) under **Planner**. Facebook can keep mirroring Instagram as it does now.
   - Post Stories on the days in between: a reply to a common question, a "2 spots left" update, a photo from your archive. Stories are where your existing followers get to know you.
   - Once a month, check **Insights** in the Instagram app for which posts got the most shares and saves, and make more of that kind.

7. **Use AI for ideas and storyboards, not for the content itself**
   - When you're stuck, ask ChatGPT or Claude: "I run small-group fly-in backpacking trips in the Yukon. Give me five Reel ideas for my 'How we do it' category, each under 30 seconds."
   - Pick one, then ask: "Turn idea 3 into a shot list of 3 to 5 second clips and a one-line on-screen hook."
   - Write the caption and voiceover yourself, the way you write your newsletter. Your voice is the thing a competitor can't copy.

#### Part C: Post Where People Search

8. **Claim YouTube and TikTok and upload your back catalogue**
   - Create a YouTube channel and a TikTok account under the name Yukon Alpine Adventures, with the same profile photo and a link to your website. On TikTok, switch to a Business account in Settings so you can schedule from a computer.
   - Upload every Reel you've already made. On YouTube, anything under three minutes in a vertical format becomes a Short.
   - Write each title as something a hiker would type into a search bar: "Fly-in backpacking trip in Kusawa Territorial Park, Yukon", "What it's like to hike off-trail in the Yukon", "Seeing caribou on a Yukon backpacking trip".
   - Pin your best single video to the top of the YouTube channel. Don't make new long-form videos for now.

9. **Schedule Shorts and TikToks in batches**
   - On YouTube, upload in YouTube Studio (studio.youtube.com) and choose **Schedule** to set a date and time. You can schedule weeks ahead, so load a month of Shorts in one sitting.
   - On TikTok, upload from a computer in TikTok Studio and switch on scheduling. TikTok only lets you schedule up to 10 days ahead, so do TikTok on each admin day.
   - Repost each week's new Reels to both channels. These platforms keep showing older videos to people who search for them, so the back catalogue keeps working.

#### Part D: Plan the 2027 Creator Trip

10. **Set aside one late-season departure as your marketing trip**
   - Choose one departure near the end of the season. Where The Light Lingers (September 4-11) is a natural fit, because a creator on a photography trip will produce strong content for a trip that needs more attention. Headwaters of the Takhini (August 24-31) is the other option.
   - Hold back one seat on that departure before it sells out and record it in your budget as marketing, along with your share of flight costs.
   - Decide now what you can offer: the trip, a fee if any, and photo and video licensing terms.

11. **Approach a creator and Air North**
   - Write to Air North first. Last year's giveaway with them worked, and a repeat partnership saves you building a new one. Ask whether they'd provide flights for a spring 2027 giveaway again.
   - Make a short list of two or three creators whose audience looks like your guests, women who hike and travel in Canada. Down Dirt Roads is the obvious first call.
   - Put the deal in writing: what they'll post during the trip (Stories and one carousel), what they'll hold back for spring (the big Reel and the giveaway post), what footage you can use, and for how long.

12. **Plan the spring giveaway**
   - Plan the giveaway for late April, when last year's ran and when late bookers for the summer are still deciding.
   - The prize is a seat on a 2028 trip plus Air North flights, so the giveaway fills next season's calendar while this season's content runs.
   - Write simple rules: entry period, who can enter, how the winner is chosen. In Canada, a prize draw needs a skill-testing question, and Instagram requires you to say the promotion isn't sponsored by Instagram. Check both before you post.
   - Schedule your own Reels from the creator trip to run in the same weeks as the creator's posts, so new followers find plenty to watch.

- Your newsletter deep-dives are ready-made carousels. One newsletter topic can become one "How we do it" carousel in 20 minutes.
- Answer every comment and message within a day during the booking window. A comment from a hiker in January is often the start of a booking.

## Tactic 3: Itinerary Pages That Get Found on Google and AI Search
Phase: Visibility
Short Label: Itinerary Pages
Subtitle: Itineraries turned into web pages, search terms in every heading and an FAQ written for the questions people ask AI tools.

### What We Heard From You
Your very first trip in 2025 was booked by someone who found you through ChatGPT, and guests are still finding you through Google and AI searches. Your detailed day-by-day itineraries, the content those tools most want to cite, sit in PDFs linked from each trip page, which search engines and AI tools largely can't read. You told us you spent a lot of time getting the homepage headings right, used AI to help with the wording, and put less time into the other pages. Your idea was to start by dropping each PDF onto its own page and improve it over time, and that's exactly the approach here.

### What This Is
You'll turn each itinerary PDF into a web page, put the search terms hikers actually use into each page's SEO title, description and headings, and grow your FAQ around the questions people type into ChatGPT when they plan a Yukon trip. Then you'll test it the way Conner showed you: ask an AI tool the questions your guests would ask, and ask it which sources it used.

### Why It Matters
Over half of travellers now use AI somewhere in their trip planning, and AI tools cite pages that answer questions clearly and in detail. A day-by-day itinerary page for a fly-in trip in Kusawa or Asi Keyi answers questions almost nobody else answers, so it has a real chance of being cited. It also warms up the people who read it, because they can picture each day before they ever write to you.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Turn the PDFs Into Pages

1. **Make your first itinerary page, starting with Kusawa**
   - In Squarespace, open the Pages panel, click **+**, add a blank page, and name it "Kusawa Territorial Park Itinerary". Set the URL slug to `/kusawa-itinerary`.
   - Copy the text from your Kusawa itinerary PDF and paste it into text blocks on the new page, one day per section.
   - Format the page title as Heading 1 ("Kusawa Territorial Park: 7-Day Guided Backpacking Itinerary") and each day as Heading 2 ("Day 1: Yukon River walk and floatplane to the alpine"). Select the text in a text block and choose the heading level from the formatting menu.
   - On the Kusawa trip page, change the "Itinerary & Additional Information" button to link to the new page instead of the PDF. Add a "Request to book" button at the bottom of the itinerary page that goes to the 2027 Departures page.
   - Keep the PDF available as a "Download a printable version" link at the bottom of the new page for guests who want it.

2. **Repeat for the other itineraries**
   - Do the same for The Boundary Ranges, Asi Keyi and Headwaters of the Takhini, one per admin session. Each takes about an hour once the first one is done.
   - Use the same structure every time: one Heading 1 that names the place and the trip type, one Heading 2 per day, and a short closing section on what's included and who the trip suits.
   - If Where The Light Lingers has an itinerary, give it a page too.
   - On the Asi Keyi page, remove the link to the 2026 booking information PDF while you're there.

3. **Add photos and alt text as you go**
   - Add one or two photos to each day, using images from that trip.
   - Click each image, open its settings and fill in the alt text field with a plain description that includes the place: "Hikers crossing an alpine ridge in Kusawa Territorial Park, Yukon".
   - Don't fill alt text with lists of keywords. One honest sentence per image is what helps both search engines and screen readers.

#### Part B: Put Search Terms in the Right Places

4. **List the searches your guests make**
   - Type the start of likely searches into Google and note what it suggests: "guided backpacking Yukon", "Kusawa Territorial Park hiking", "Asi Keyi Territorial Park", "fly-in hiking trip Yukon", "Kluane backpacking trip", "Takhini River".
   - In Squarespace, open **Analytics** and find **Search Keywords**. Follow the prompt to connect Google Search Console if it isn't connected yet. After a few weeks it shows the actual searches that bring people to your site.
   - Write one main search term per page: the homepage, the Expeditions page, each trip page and each new itinerary page.

5. **Rewrite each page's SEO title and description**
   - For each page, open its settings (the gear icon in the Pages panel), go to **SEO**, and write an SEO title of 50 to 60 characters that starts with the main search term. For example: "Kusawa Territorial Park Guided Backpacking Trip | Yukon Alpine Adventures".
   - Write an SEO description of about 150 characters that says who the trip is for and what makes it different.
   - Fix The Boundary Ranges first. Its search description is currently copied from the Kusawa page, so Google shows the wrong trip.
   - Your homepage title already does this well. Use it as the model for every other page.

6. **Use your search terms in headings on every trip page**
   - On each trip page, make sure there is one Heading 1 and at least two Heading 2s, and that at least one heading includes the main search term, for example "A Guided Backpacking Expedition Through the Boundary Ranges".
   - Some of your current subheadings are bold body text rather than headings. Select each one and set it to Heading 2 so search engines read it as a heading.
   - Keep the headings readable. If a heading sounds awkward when you say it out loud, rewrite it.

#### Part C: Answer the Questions AI Tools Get Asked

7. **Run the AI search test**
   - Open ChatGPT in a private (incognito) browser window, without logging in, so it doesn't know who you are.
   - Ask the questions your guests would ask: "Help me plan a guided backpacking trip in the Yukon", "What are the best small-group hiking expeditions in the Yukon?", "Is there a guided fly-in hiking trip in Kluane or Asi Keyi?", "Is a guided backpacking trip in the Yukon a good idea for a solo woman?"
   - Note whether you're mentioned, and which companies are. Then ask: "Which sources did you use for that answer?" Push for specifics if it hedges.
   - Repeat the same questions in Google's AI answers and any other AI tool you use. Save the results in one document so you can compare them later.

8. **Add the FAQs people ask before they book**
   - Your FAQ page already answers 11 questions well, from fitness to mosquitoes. Add the questions you answer most often by email.
   - Good additions: What's included in the price, and what are the transaction fees? How does booking work, and when do I pay? What is your cancellation policy? Can I join as a solo traveller? How do I get to Whitehorse, and where do we meet? What gear do I need to bring or rent? Which trip suits my experience? Whose traditional territory do we travel through?
   - Write each answer in two to four plain sentences, in your own voice. Start each answer with the direct answer, then the detail. AI tools quote first sentences.

9. **Act on what the AI test showed you**
   - If the AI tools cited TripAdvisor or Google reviews, make sure your TripAdvisor listing is claimed and points to your 2027 Departures page.
   - If they cited Travel Yukon, update your Travel Yukon listing with your 2027 trips and the link to your departures page.
   - If they cited Reddit or a blog, note which ones, and answer honestly where you can as yourself, the owner, without selling.
   - At the end of the 90 days, run the same questions again and compare with your first results.

### Tips
- Do one page per sitting and finish it. A fully done Kusawa page is worth more than five half-done pages.
- When you write a blog post or a newsletter deep-dive, link it to the matching itinerary page. Links between your own pages help search engines understand which page matters most.
- Write itinerary days the way you'd tell a friend about them. Specific details (the lake, the river, the animals you usually see) are what AI tools pick up and what readers remember.
- Don't pay for AI visibility scoring tools. The incognito test gives you the same information for free.

## GBP Quick Wins

### On the Google Business Profile
- **Add the share link to your post-trip emails.** Search for Yukon Alpine Adventures while logged in, use the review or share option on your profile to copy its link, and put it in your post-trip and end-of-season emails in place of the request to share on Instagram. One clear ask gets more reviews than three.
- **Upload a fresh batch of photos.** Add 10 to 15 of your best trip photos now, then a few new ones every month. Fresh photos show Google the business is active, and they push your own images ahead of whatever guests upload.
- **Fill in every attribute.** Open **Edit profile** and work through every attribute and detail section that applies to you. Each one is another way to match a search.
- **Check your address is hidden.** Under the location settings in **Edit profile**, make sure your home address is not shown and that your service area covers Whitehorse and the regions you guide in.
- **List your expeditions as services.** Add each 2027 trip as a service with a one-line description, so people see what you offer straight from the search results.

### On the Website
- **Make your phone number tappable.** Link your phone number on the contact page and in the footer with a tel: link, so people on a phone can call you in one tap.
- **Fix the duplicate main heading.** Change the second Heading 1 on your homepage, "Get ready for 2027", to Heading 2, so the page has one clear main heading.
- **Add alt text to the homepage images.** Seven images on your site have no alt text. Add a plain one-sentence description to each, naming the place where you can.
- **Check every trip page loads.** Open each trip from the Expeditions menu on a phone and a computer. The Where The Light Lingers page returned an error when we checked it, so start there.
