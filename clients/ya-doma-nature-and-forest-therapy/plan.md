## Goal
In the next 90 days, you will get Ya Doma ready to relaunch in March 2027. By the end of week 12, your forest therapy walk will be written down from welcome to tea ceremony, it will have a price you have worked out rather than guessed, and someone who finds you on Google, Instagram or Tourism Harrison will be able to understand it and book a date in a few clicks.

This plan is about clarity and the booking path, not about reach. Your website already gets 5 to 6 visits a day from around the world, and your 5 Google reviews are all five stars. The people are finding you. What is missing is a clear offer, a price, and a simple way to say yes. Today, bookings arrive by phone and email with no system behind them, and people who inquire often book and then cancel. Fixing that comes before any extra promotion, because more visitors to an unclear page only means more emails to answer.

Revenue will not arrive inside these 90 days, because you are not running walks until the relaunch. What you will have at week 12 is a walk that is bookable online with set dates, a website that leads straight to it, Google Analytics collecting data from day one, a complete Google Business Profile ready to reopen, and the pricing worksheet that tells you how many walks a month it takes to reach your goal of working fully for yourself. That number becomes the target for 2027.

The plan is built around one day a week. Each week has two to four jobs, and none of them need technical skills beyond copying, pasting and filling in forms in Wix and Google.

## Strategic Positioning
Ya Doma is not a hike, a spa day or a nature tour. It is a slow, guided forest therapy walk led by a registered clinical counsellor, with a beginning, a middle and an end: a welcome and settling in, a silent walk where your feet slow down, the Pleasures of Presence meditation, a series of invitations with optional sharing, and a hemlock tea ceremony where you talk about what you noticed and how to carry it into everyday life. Guests leave with an experience and with a practice they can return to on their own.

Your counselling training is what separates this from a guided walk in the woods. It is why you can hold space for people moving through grief, loss, anxiety and big life transitions, and why the closing integration matters as much as the walk itself. That is also why the word "therapy" stays in the name. The work in this plan is to explain it so a visitor never reads "therapy" and thinks "I don't need that." Lead with what the guest experiences and what they walk away with, and let the depth show in how you describe it.

You serve two audiences. Visitors to Harrison Hot Springs are looking for something meaningful to do while they are here, and they book once and tell their friends. Local people come back, often starting with your Sunday meditation. The meditation is the easiest first step into a relationship with you, and the walk is the experience you want every meditation guest to try. This plan puts the walk at the centre of everything, and treats the meditation as the way in.

It is not for people who want a fast-paced outing, a photo stop or a fitness hike. Saying so plainly helps the right people book and keeps the wrong people from cancelling.

## Tactic 1: Define the Forest Therapy Walk and Make It Bookable
Phase: Foundation
Short Label: Walk & Booking
Subtitle: Write down your walk, set a price that sustains you, and give guests a simple way to book and prepare.

### What We Heard From You
You told us people "don't quite understand" your offerings, and that you get inquiries and bookings that later cancel. Pricing has moved from $75 for three hours to around $140, and you said yourself you don't have a settled price point. Bookings come in by phone or email with no system for scheduling, payment or tracking guests, and the emails are hard to keep up with. You also told us you want two things at once: a schedule people can simply book into without talking to you, and a conversation first for guests who need a tailored experience. You want to keep the meeting place, parking and logistics in a personal welcome email rather than on the website. You put the business on pause in 2025 and are using this program to prepare for a relaunch in March 2027.

### What This Is
This tactic turns the walk you already run into a clear, bookable experience. You will write a run sheet of the walk, describe in one or two sentences what guests walk away with, decide which walks you will offer at relaunch and how long they are, and set prices using the eLearningU pricing tool. Then you will set up Wix Bookings so guests can book a scheduled walk directly or request a private one, and write a small set of email templates that you personalise rather than rewrite each time.

### Why It Matters
Every other tactic in this plan sends people to your booking page, so the offer has to be clear and the booking has to work before anything else. A written run sheet and a clear price also answer the two questions that cause most hesitation and cancellations: "What actually happens?" and "Is it worth it?" Standard emails mean each guest still feels looked after, without you starting from a blank page every time.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Define the Experience

1. **Write your walk run sheet**
   - Open a blank document and title it "Ya Doma Forest Therapy Walk: Run Sheet".
   - Write the walk as a sequence, with a rough time for each part: arrival and welcome (introductions, bathrooms, what to expect), the quiet walk or the nature story share, slow walking to the sit spot, Pleasures of Presence, each invitation and its sharing circle, the tea ceremony, and the closing conversation about processing and integration.
   - Add what happens before the walk (the booking, the welcome email, any conversation) and after it (the thank-you email, a check-in, an invitation to come back).
   - Mark the two or three moments guests mention most afterwards. From what you described, the tea ceremony and the moment "something clicks" when people walk out of the forest are likely candidates. These become the moments you describe on your website.
   - Keep it to one or two pages. This is your reference, not a script.

2. **Write what guests walk away with**
   - Write one or two sentences that describe the change a guest feels after the walk, in plain words a visitor would use. For example, the shape might be: "You'll slow down, notice what's going on around you and inside you, and leave with a simple practice you can use at home."
   - Write a second version that names who it is especially for, using only the words you are comfortable with in public, such as people moving through grief, loss or a big life change.
   - Keep clinical language for your private conversations and intake. On public pages, describe the experience and the feeling, not a diagnosis.
   - Read both versions out loud. If a stranger in a hurry would understand them, you're done. Save them, because they will go on your homepage, your Google profile and your Tourism Harrison listing.

3. **Choose your relaunch lineup and walk lengths**
   - Decide which offers will be bookable in March 2027. A focused lineup is easier to explain: one scheduled small-group walk, one private walk, and the Sunday meditation as a low-cost first step.
   - Decide whether the family walk, the 8-week group program, individual sessions and Reiki are on the website at relaunch or added later in 2027. Anything you are not ready to run should come off the booking pages for now.
   - Set a published length you can always honour. You told us most guests choose two hours and you usually run to two and a half, so publish the length the walk actually takes. Guests trust a walk that ends when you said it would.
   - Set a group size for the scheduled walk, for example up to six people, and a minimum below which you will reschedule.

#### Part B: Set Your Prices

4. **Work out your costs and price with the pricing tool**
   - Go to pricing.elearningu.com, click New Experience and choose the Walking Tour template.
   - Enter your guests per walk and how many walks you plan to run in 2027.
   - Under delivery costs, enter a guide fee for your own time based on the minimum hourly rate you need to keep running the business, not a token amount. Add your insurance and website costs, and the cost of tea per guest.
   - Leave overhead at about 10 percent unless you know your real figure.
   - Note your break-even price per guest, then add a value premium. Your counselling work is billed at $150 an hour, which is a useful reference point for what your time is worth.
   - Write down the final price for each format, and the number of walks per month you need to reach your goal of working fully for yourself.

5. **Decide how you will keep it accessible**
   - Choose one approach rather than discounting case by case. Options you raised: a lower Harrison resident rate, a group rate when someone brings friends, or one walk a month offered by donation.
   - The pricing tool also lets you add a community giving contribution, such as a few dollars from every walk going to a cause you choose. Use it to check that each option still leaves you above break-even.
   - Write a single sentence for your booking page that explains your approach, so nobody has to ask.

6. **Write your cancellation and weather policy**
   - Decide how far ahead a guest can cancel for a full refund or a free reschedule, for example 72 hours.
   - Decide what happens when you cancel for unsafe conditions such as high wind, snow or ice: a full refund or a free reschedule. Rain alone is part of the forest in Harrison, so say whether walks go ahead in the rain and what to wear.
   - Keep the policy to four or five sentences. It will go into Wix Bookings and your confirmation email.

#### Part C: Build the Booking System in Wix

7. **Set up your scheduled walk as a Wix Bookings class**
   - In your Wix dashboard, go to Booking Services. If Wix Bookings isn't installed, add it from the Wix App Market first.
   - Click to add a new service and choose Class. A class is a group session at a set time with a set number of places, which suits a scheduled walk.
   - Name it with the walk name from your run sheet, add your description, set the length, the maximum number of guests and the price from Step 4.
   - For location, choose a custom location and write "Near Harrison Hot Springs. Exact meeting point sent after booking." so the trail stays private.
   - Go to Booking Calendar, click the date and time of your first relaunch walk, choose Class Session and select this walk. Add at least four dates for March and April 2027.
   - To take payment online, go to Settings, then Accept Payments, and connect a payment provider. If your Wix plan doesn't include online payments, set the walk to pay in person for now and add online payment when you upgrade.

8. **Set up private walks as appointment requests**
   - In Booking Services, add a second service and choose Appointment. Name it, for example, "Private Forest Therapy Walk".
   - Under Booking requests, select Manually approve or decline booking requests. Guests send a request, and nothing is confirmed until you have had your conversation and approved it.
   - Set your available days so requests only come in for times you can walk.
   - Add the same price logic and the same custom location wording as Step 7.

9. **Add your intake questions to the booking forms**
   - In each service, find the booking form section and choose to create a custom booking form.
   - Keep the questions short and practical: name, email, phone, how they heard about Ya Doma (this answers the question you told us you've never asked), any mobility or accessibility needs, and "Is there anything you'd like me to know before we walk together?"
   - Do not ask for mental health history on a web form. Invite guests who want a tailored walk to say so, and have that conversation by phone or in your welcome email.
   - Add your cancellation and weather policy from Step 6 under Booking Settings, then Booking Policies, and connect it to both services.

10. **Test the booking path end to end**
   - Publish the site, then on your phone, book a walk on your own booking page as if you were a guest.
   - Check that you received the confirmation email, that the booking shows in your Booking Calendar, and that the location wording does not reveal the trail.
   - Send a private walk request and check that it appears under Pending Booking Requests in your dashboard, then decline it.
   - Ask a friend to do the same test on their phone and tell you anywhere they hesitated.

#### Part D: Standardise Your Guest Emails

11. **Write your welcome and logistics email**
   - Write one email you send personally after every confirmed booking. Include the meeting point and parking, the start time and how early to arrive, what to wear (layers, rain jacket, closed-toe shoes), what to bring (water), the expected length, and what happens if the weather turns.
   - Add a short paragraph on what to expect emotionally, in your own voice: everything is an invitation, sharing is optional, and the pace is slow.
   - In Gmail, go to Settings, See all settings, Advanced, and turn on Templates. Write the email, then click the three dots at the bottom of the message, choose Templates, then Save draft as template.
   - Each time, insert the template and add one personal line before sending.

12. **Write your thank-you and review email**
   - Write a warm thank-you email to send the day after each walk. Include one line reflecting on the walk, a gentle reminder of the integration practice, and the date of the next Sunday meditation or walk.
   - Add one optional sentence asking for a review, with the Google review link from Tactic 3. Make it clearly optional.
   - Only send review requests to forest therapy walk and meditation guests. Keep your counselling clients completely separate from review requests.
   - Save it as a Gmail template the same way as Step 11.

13. **Save replies for the questions you get most**
   - List the five questions people ask most by email, for example "What is forest therapy?", "Is it suitable for me if I'm grieving?", "How long is it?", "Can I bring my partner?" and "What if it rains?".
   - Write a short answer to each and save each as a Gmail template.
   - Each answer should end by pointing to your booking page, so the conversation moves towards a date.

### Tips
- Write the run sheet the way you would explain it to a friend over tea. You don't need perfect wording, just the order and the feeling.
- Conner's pricing advice: early walks can be priced lower while you gather feedback, then raise the price until roughly half the people you speak to say it's too much. If nobody says no, you are too cheap.
- Wix can also send an automatic email when a session ends. Start with sending the thank-you yourself, since the personal touch matters to your guests, and automate later if volume grows.
- If you find yourself repeating an answer by email more than twice, turn it into a saved reply or add it to your website.

## Tactic 2: A Website That Says What It Is and Books It
Phase: Conversion
Short Label: Website Clarity
Subtitle: A homepage and service pages that tell visitors what the walk is, what it costs and how to book it.

### What We Heard From You
On our coaching call, we spent real time on your website and social and still weren't sure what the offer was until you explained it. The Forest Therapy Services page is the strongest page on the site, but it has no price, no itinerary and no button to book. The homepage presents forest therapy, counselling and Reiki side by side, and services like the family walk read as general content rather than something you can book. The homepage still carries a notice that Ya Doma is closed for all services in 2026, which the assessment found is also tagged as a second main heading alongside "Sunday Morning Meditation". There is no Google Analytics, so you can see visits in Wix but not where they come from.

### What This Is
This tactic rewrites the path from your homepage to a confirmed booking. The homepage gets one clear message and one button. Each service page gets the same simple structure: what it is, an itinerary, the price, and a Book Now button connected to Wix Bookings. Then you'll do a few search housekeeping jobs: Google Analytics, one main heading per page, business details markup that leaves your home address out, and a single reference sheet so your name and description match everywhere.

### Why It Matters
Most people look at your site on a phone with something else competing for their attention. If they can't see what the walk is, what it costs and how to book in a few seconds, they leave. The service page copy is already good, so you aren't rewriting it, just adding the missing pieces. The search work is small, but it helps Google show the right page to the right people, and Google Analytics only records data from the day it is installed.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Homepage

1. **Replace the closure notice with a relaunch message**
   - In the Wix editor, open your homepage and click the "closed for all services in 2026" text.
   - Replace it with a short relaunch line, for example "Forest therapy walks return in March 2027. Booking is now open." Adjust the wording once your dates are live.
   - With the text selected, open the text style dropdown and set it to a paragraph style or Heading 2, not Heading 1. This also fixes the duplicate main heading the assessment found.

2. **Rewrite the top of the homepage around one message and one button**
   - The first screen should answer three questions: who you are, what you offer and what's in it for the visitor. Use your statement from Tactic 1, Step 2.
   - Add one button directly under it, for example "Book a Forest Therapy Walk", linked to your Forest Therapy Services page or straight to your Wix Bookings walk.
   - Check the first screen on your phone in the editor's mobile view. The message and the button should be visible without scrolling.
   - Leave the rest of the homepage copy as it is for now.

3. **Put the walk first in your services**
   - On the homepage, list the forest therapy walk first, with the Sunday meditation as the low-cost way to start.
   - Move counselling and Reiki further down the page, or onto their own page, so a visitor isn't asked to choose between three different things before they understand the first one.
   - Remove or hide any service you decided not to offer at relaunch in Tactic 1, Step 3.

#### Part B: Service Pages

4. **Add an itinerary, price and Book button to the walk page**
   - Keep your existing Forest Therapy Services copy. Under each walk, add a short itinerary from your run sheet in four or five lines: welcome, quiet walk, Pleasures of Presence, invitations, tea ceremony.
   - Add the price and the length directly under the itinerary, plus your one-sentence accessibility approach from Tactic 1, Step 5.
   - Add a Book Now button under each walk. In the editor, click Add Elements, then Button, then click Link and connect it to the matching Wix Bookings service. If you prefer the dates to show on the page, add the Wix Bookings calendar element instead.
   - Repeat the button at the bottom of the page so nobody has to scroll back up.

5. **Use the same format for every other live service**
   - For each service still offered at relaunch (for example the family walk or the Sunday meditation), add a heading that names it as a service, two or three lines on what it is, the length, the price and a Book Now or Get in Touch button.
   - For services that need a conversation first, such as individual sessions, make the button go to a short contact form or the private walk request from Tactic 1, Step 8.

6. **Add a "What to Expect" section**
   - Add a short section to the walk page, or a separate page linked from it, answering the questions from Tactic 1, Step 13: what forest therapy is, who it suits, how fit you need to be, what to wear, what happens in the rain, and whether sharing is required.
   - Include accessibility information about the terrain and pace.
   - Say that the exact meeting point and parking details are sent after booking. Do not publish the trail location.

7. **Remove your home address and exact location from the site**
   - Check every page, the footer and the Contact page for your home street address or a map pin on your home or the trail.
   - Replace any address with "Harrison Hot Springs, BC" and your phone and email.
   - In your dashboard, go to Settings, then Business Info, and check what is entered as your business location. Wix uses this for the business details it shows to search engines, so it should not be your home street address.

#### Part C: Search and Measurement

8. **Install Google Analytics 4**
   - Go to analytics.google.com and sign in with your business Google account. Click Admin, then Create, then Property. Name it "Ya Doma", choose Canada and Canadian dollars.
   - When asked for a data stream, choose Web, enter www.yadoma.ca and click Create stream. Copy the Measurement ID, which starts with "G-".
   - In your Wix dashboard, go to Marketing Integrations and click Connect under Google Tag (on some sites this card is called Google Analytics). Paste the Measurement ID and save.
   - Open your website on your phone, then in Google Analytics go to Reports, then Realtime. You should see one active user within a minute or two.

9. **Check there is only one main heading per page**
   - In the Wix editor, go through each page and click on each large title. The text style dropdown shows whether it is Heading 1.
   - Each page should have exactly one Heading 1, describing what the page is about. Change any others to Heading 2 or Heading 3.
   - Start with the homepage, where the assessment found two.

10. **Add business details markup without your home address**
   - Go to search.google.com/test/rich-results, enter www.yadoma.ca and run the test. Look for "Local business" in the results and check whether it shows a street address.
   - In the Wix editor, click Pages & Menu, click the three dots next to your homepage, choose SEO basics, then the Advanced SEO tab, then Structured Data Markup. If Wix already added a Local Business markup, edit it. If not, click + Add New Markup.
   - Use your own details and leave out a street address and map coordinates: `{"@context": "https://schema.org", "@type": "LocalBusiness", "name": "Ya Doma Nature and Forest Therapy", "url": "https://www.yadoma.ca", "telephone": "+1-604-378-0369", "email": "yadomaforesttherapy@gmail.com", "areaServed": "Harrison Hot Springs, BC", "description": "Your description from Step 11"}`
   - Replace the description text with your real description, save, publish, and run the Rich Results Test again to check there are no errors. If anything looks wrong, bring a screenshot to your next coaching call.

11. **Create your business details sheet**
   - Make one document with your exact business name, a short description (one or two sentences), a longer description (up to 750 characters, for Google), your phone number written one way, your email, website and service area.
   - Use it every time you fill in a profile: website, Google Business Profile, Instagram and Tourism Harrison. Google trusts a business more when the name and description match everywhere.
   - Use "Ya Doma Nature and Forest Therapy" as the name in every listing.

12. **Update the homepage search description**
   - In the Wix editor, click Pages & Menu, click the three dots next to Home, choose SEO basics, and find the meta description.
   - Replace the current 240-character description with one under 155 characters, built from your short description and ending with a reason to click, for example "Book a guided forest therapy walk near Harrison Hot Springs."
   - Leave your page title as it is. It already includes a search term people use.

## Tactic 3: Google Profile, Reviews and Relaunch Visibility
Phase: Visibility
Short Label: Google & Relaunch
Subtitle: A complete Google profile, a gentle review habit and a clear relaunch message to the people who already know you.

### What We Heard From You
You created your Google Business Profile but told us you "don't know what I'm doing at all" with it. It is currently marked temporarily closed and set up as a region rather than a street address, which is the right setup because you work from home and don't want people knowing where you take them. You have 5 Google reviews, all five stars, and 965 Instagram followers, with posting at about one or two a month. You told us Tourism Harrison were your biggest supporter and probably your main source of bookings. Your Sunday meditations drew a small, loyal group of locals at $10, and you already send a thank-you note after sessions.

### What This Is
This tactic gets you ready to be found when you reopen. You will complete your Google Business Profile as a service-area business, add photos and a booking link, and reopen it when bookings are live. You will set up a simple, optional review request for walk guests. You will update your Tourism Harrison listing and let them know you're coming back. Finally, you'll start a small email list from the people who already know you, record a few short clips explaining the walk in your own voice, and announce the relaunch.

### Why It Matters
Google shows more complete profiles to more people, and a profile marked closed ranks below open businesses. Reviews are the first thing a wellness visitor checks, and five is a small number to carry a business, so a steady, gentle habit matters more than a one-time push. Your meditation guests and past walkers are your warmest audience. A personal note to them is worth more than any number of posts.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Google Business Profile

1. **Confirm access and your service area**
   - On your computer, sign in to the Google account you used to create the profile, then search Google for "Ya Doma Nature and Forest Therapy". Your profile should appear with an Edit profile button.
   - Click Edit profile, then Location. Check that "Show business address to customers" is turned off.
   - Next to Service area, click the pencil and add the areas you serve, for example Harrison Hot Springs, Agassiz and Harrison Mills. Click Save.
   - If you can't see Edit profile, you may be signed in with a different Google account. Bring this to your next coaching call.

2. **Update your description, categories and services**
   - Click Edit profile, then About. Paste your longer description from your business details sheet (Tactic 2, Step 11).
   - Check your primary category. Search the category list for one that describes the walk (for example "Wellness program"), and add secondary categories only for services you are offering at relaunch.
   - Add each relaunch service with a short description and its price.
   - Add your Book a Walk page as the booking or appointment link under Contact, and add your opening year (2020).

3. **Add 10 to 20 photos**
   - From your profile, click Add photo and upload 10 to 20 pictures: the forest in different seasons, the tea ceremony set up, hands holding a cup, feet on moss, a small group walking from behind, and one clear photo of you.
   - Only use photos of guests if you have their written permission. Photos from behind or of hands and details work well and protect privacy.
   - Avoid any photo that shows a trail sign, a parking area or another landmark that would give away the location.

4. **Reopen your profile when bookings are live**
   - Once your first relaunch dates are bookable on the website, click Edit profile, then Hours.
   - Change the status from Temporarily closed to open. Because walks run by booking, choose "Open with no main hours" unless you prefer to list regular hours.
   - Search for your business the next day to check the "Temporarily closed" label has gone.

#### Part B: Reviews

5. **Get your review link and set up the request**
   - In your profile, click Ask for reviews and copy the short link.
   - Paste it into your thank-you email template from Tactic 1, Step 12.
   - Optionally, make a small card with a QR code for the link that you can give to guests at the end of the tea ceremony, only when it feels right. Free QR code generators will turn the link into an image you can print.
   - Ask walk and meditation guests only. Your counselling clients stay outside this process entirely.

6. **Invite a small group to a pilot walk**
   - Invite five or six Sunday meditation regulars or people who have walked with you before to a pilot walk at a reduced or no-cost price, before your public dates.
   - Afterwards, ask them three questions: what stood out, what was unclear before they arrived, and what would make the walk worth more to them.
   - Use their answers to adjust your walk page, and send them the thank-you email with the review link.

#### Part C: Tourism Harrison and Partners

7. **Update your Tourism Harrison listing**
   - Contact Tourism Harrison and ask them to update your listing with the name, short description, booking link and photos from your business details sheet.
   - Make sure the business name and phone number match your website and Google profile exactly.

8. **Tell Tourism Harrison about the relaunch**
   - Send a short note telling them your March 2027 relaunch dates, the walk, the price and the booking link.
   - Offer to take a staff member on the pilot walk if they haven't walked with you recently, so front-line staff can describe it to visitors.
   - Ask whether they have spring itineraries or social posts the walk could be part of.

#### Part D: Relaunch Story and Email List

9. **Start a small email list from the people who know you**
   - Make a list of past Sunday meditation guests and walk guests whose email addresses you have.
   - Send them a short personal note asking whether they'd like to hear about new walks and meditations. Only add people who say yes, since Canada's anti-spam law requires permission before you send marketing emails.
   - Add a simple sign-up form to your website footer so new people can join.
   - When meditations restart, ask every guest if they'd like to join the list.

10. **Record three or four short clips explaining the walk**
   - On a quiet morning in the forest, film 10 to 15 short clips: walking slowly, light through the trees, the tea being made, hands on bark. Avoid anything that shows the location.
   - Record a voiceover for three or four of them in your own words: what the walk is, what happens during it, why you started Ya Doma, and what guests walk away with. Share only as much of your own story as you are comfortable with.
   - In Instagram, create a Reel from the clips, then use the voiceover option in the Reel editor to record over them.
   - Each post should end with the same simple call to action: "Book a walk at yadoma.ca".

11. **Announce the relaunch**
   - Send a personal email to your list with your relaunch dates, the walk description, the price and the booking link.
   - Post one of your clips on Instagram with the same message, and update your Instagram bio to say walks return in March 2027 with your booking link.
   - Plan to post about one clip a week leading up to and through March.

### Tips
- Reviews will pick up once walks restart. Aim for steady progress, one or two a month, towards 15 by the end of your first season, rather than chasing a big number quickly.
- Always reply to new reviews within a few days. A short, warm reply that mentions something specific shows future guests you care.
- You don't have to appear on camera. Your voice over forest footage carries a lot.
- Batch your clips. One morning of filming gives you a month or more of posts.
