Your 90-day strategy

Your goal

Andrea's Resort is a lakeside cabin property with a perfect 5.0 Google rating, a working online booking system and 37 strong photos on its website. What it does not have is visibility. There are only 20 reviews, 522 followers across Instagram and Facebook, and one social post every ten weeks.

Over the next 90 days the goal is to increase direct bookings by 50% and grow the social following past 1,000, by posting consistently, building a steady flow of new reviews, and making the resort easy to find in local search.

Your positioning

Most cabin properties in the region compete on price or on the size of the hot tub. Andrea's Resort competes on calm: a quiet stretch of lakefront, cabins that are properly looked after, and owners who answer the phone.

“Andrea's Resort is the lakeside escape for families and couples who want a peaceful, nature-first getaway with modern comforts and personal hospitality. Every post, review reply and web page should sound like the owners talking, and should show the lake.”

Your four objectives

Build Social Presence

A consistent, engaging social presence that shows the cabin experience and reaches new audiences.

Reviews · weeks 3 to 8

Win Direct Bookings

Tactic 1 · Foundation

Social Media Activation

Build a consistent, engaging presence that drives discovery and turns followers into guests.

What this is

A four-week sprint that takes your social media from dormant to active. You will set a posting rhythm, turn the photos you already own into a month of posts in one sitting, start publishing short video, and build the small daily habits that grow an audience.

What we heard from you

You have beautiful cabin photography and guests who love their stay, but your social accounts do not show it. With 194 followers on Instagram, 328 on Facebook and one post every ten weeks, the accounts look closed. You told us the blocker is time, not ideas.

“The blocker is time, not ideas.”

Why it matters

Leisure travellers find places to stay on Instagram and Facebook before they ever search Google. A quiet account suggests a quiet business. Three posts a week for twelve weeks beats ten posts in one burst followed by a month of silence.

Tips

  • Post when your audience is online: 7 to 9 in the morning and 7 to 9 in the evening.
  • Follower count matters less than engagement rate and website clicks. A small audience that responds books more often than a large one that scrolls past.
  • If a week gets away from you, post one photo of the lake with one honest sentence. Keeping the rhythm matters more than the quality of any single post.

Tactic 2 · Growth

Review Generation

Turn happy guests into steady, recent reviews.

What this is

A simple system that asks every guest once, at the right moment, and makes it take under a minute.

What we heard from you

You have a perfect 5.0 on Google, but only 20 reviews, and almost none from this year. You have never asked guests for one because it felt awkward.

“Asking for a review has always felt awkward.”

Why it matters

Review count and recency both affect local ranking. A property with 70 recent reviews at 4.8 outranks one with 20 older reviews at 5.0.

Tactic 3 · Search Visibility

Local Search Visibility and the Pages That Earn Search Traffic All Year

Be the first result when someone nearby searches for a lakeside cabin.

What this is

Twelve weeks of small, specific jobs: complete the Google profile, fix the technical basics on the website, list the resort consistently across directories, and publish the pages that answer what guests already ask.

What we heard from you

Your Google Business Profile is verified but barely used. The website has no structured data, the homepage title is cut off in search results, and the resort is missing from most tourism directories. You also told us that guests ask the same five questions by email every week.

“Guests email us the same five questions every week.”

Why it matters

Someone searching "lakeside cabin near me" is ready to book. Local search is where the resort's perfect rating can do the most work, and right now very few people see it.

Tactic 4 · Booking and Retention

Direct Booking and Guest Email

Make booking direct the easiest option, and stay in touch with guests who have already stayed.

What this is

Two connected pieces of work: remove the friction from booking on your own website, and start a small, respectful email program for past guests.

What we heard from you

Most of your bookings come through third-party sites that take a commission, even from guests who found you by name. You have email addresses for several years of past guests and have never written to them.

“We have years of guest emails and have never written to them.”

Why it matters

Every booking that moves from a third-party site to your own website keeps the commission in the business. Past guests are the cheapest bookings you will ever get, because they already know the place.

Success metrics

Metric30 Days60 Days90 Days
Direct share of bookingsBaseline recordedUp 15%Up 50%

Tips

  • Describe the emails to guests as service, not marketing: one before they arrive and one when dates open.
  • Offer something small for booking direct, such as a late checkout, and say so beside the Book Now button.

Notes

Google profile and website wins

Each of these takes less than an hour, and you can start any of them today without waiting on the rest of the plan.

On the Google Business Profile

Reply to all 20 Google reviews. None of them has a response. Reply to each one personally and mention something the guest said.
Add seasonal opening dates and check-in hours. A traveller planning a February trip has no way to know whether you are open.
Start Google Posts. One post every two weeks keeps the listing active in local results.

On the Website

Shorten the homepage title. It is cut off in search results. Keep it under 60 characters.
Add the phone number to the header. Make it a tappable link on mobile.
